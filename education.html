Girls' Education

Education for Girls

The Taliban ban on girls' education beyond grade 6 has placed millions of Afghan girls in a precarious position. HHRDO's girls' education program is more critical than ever.

Bibi Female School: Operating since 2018, this school serves 400+ students ages 5-18. Initially teaching grades 1-3, it has advanced to grade 8 as of 2024/2025. The school aspires to reach 12th grade, preparing students for the university entry exam (Kankur).

Siddiqa Ulfat School: Launched in 2022 in Kohistan, Kapisa, this school serves 130 students with a focus on Islamic studies, gradually implementing the Bibi School model despite funding constraints.

English & Computer Classes: Our intensive 1-year program for girls 16+ focuses on English and Computer Science. To date, 85 girls are enrolled, with 25 having graduated from all three levels of English instruction.

Specialized Training

Individual Courses

Beyond full-time schooling, we offer focused skill-development programs:

  • English Classes for Female Students: Intensive language instruction designed for girls seeking communication and professional skills
  • English Classes for Male Students: Comprehensive English proficiency programs for boys of all skill levels
  • Computer Classes: Digital literacy and computer skills training for both boys and girls, essential in today's job market

These courses run throughout the year and provide flexible scheduling for students balancing work and education.

Future Growth

Online Bachelor Degree Initiative

HHRDO is pioneering online university education for Afghan students, removing barriers to higher learning.

Pathway to Online Bachelor Degree:

  1. Complete HHRDO 2-year Computer Science Diploma
  2. Achieve minimum 75 score on TOEFL English test
  3. Apply to partner universities (USA, UK, India, Norway, Sweden)
  4. Secure government grants for tuition
  5. Complete online Bachelor's degree from home in Afghanistan

Key Benefits: 100% retention of educated graduates in Afghanistan, direct aid to students without bureaucracy, women from conservative areas can participate, affordable compared to traditional scholarships.
